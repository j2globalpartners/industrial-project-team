- Incoterms 2020: eleven rules [V]; seven for any mode of transport (EXW, FCA, CPT, CIP, DAP, DPU, DDP) and four maritime (FAS, FOB, CFR, CIF); DPU replaces DAT [S]. Consistent with the sheet.
- **Risk passes from seller to buyer at the same point and at the same moment as delivery**; only two rules, CIP and CIF, oblige the seller to provide insurance for the buyer [V, ICC Academy].
- Minimum insurance cover rises to "all risks" under CIP and stays at the minimum level under CIF [S].
- **Incoterms never govern the transfer of title** [S].
- Three families of risk transfer: the buyer arranges the main carriage (FCA, FAS, FOB); the seller arranges it but risk passes beforehand (CPT, CIP, CFR, CIF); the seller arranges it and risk passes on arrival (DAP, DPU, DDP) [S].
- World Bank: value for money is the optimum combination of total cost and quality; rated criteria are used to evaluate the non-price attributes of bids [V].
- Expediting and inspection: no authoritative reference found [NC].

- Constructability, Construction Industry Institute definition: "the optimum use of construction knowledge and experience in planning, engineering, procurement and field operations to achieve overall project objectives" [V, quoted by IPA]. The constructability review is the most widely used value-improving practice according to IPA [V].
- Advanced work packaging (AWP): an installation work package groups the tasks of one crew for one shift, with the documents and materials needed [E].
- Gains announced by the CII for AWP: up to 10% lower installed cost and 25% higher productivity — these are ceilings, not averages, and the report has not been read [NC]. Do not turn them into an anchor.
- Collaborative planning (Last Planner): rolling look-ahead plan over a few weeks with constraint removal, weekly plan, measurement of the share of promised tasks actually completed [S; no source from the issuing body].

- EN 10204:2004, four documents: 2.1 declaration of compliance without results; 2.2 test report with results of non-specific inspection; 3.1 certificate validated by a manufacturer's representative independent of manufacturing; 3.2 additionally validated by the purchaser's representative or a third party [S]. Consistent with the sheet.

- Welder qualification (ISO 9606-1): revalidation by a new test every three years, or every two years on evidence of two test welds examined (radiography, ultrasonics or destructive tests) within the preceding six months, or without limit under conditions [V, TWI technical note]. Six-monthly confirmation by the welding coordinator [S].

- IEC 62337: sequence of construction, pre-commissioning, cold then hot commissioning, production; milestones of completion of erection, mechanical completion, start-up, performance test, acceptance [E].
- Pre-commissioning is the phase of testing with test fluids (water, inerts), before any process material; during the performance test, the owner's personnel operate the plant under the contractor's supervision [E].
- IEC 62382 covers the period between the end of loop construction and the start of cold commissioning; it says what to test, not how [E].
- Commissioning according to the UK regulator: it is "the practical test of the adequacy of prior preparations", operator training and operating instructions included; commissioning procedures describe a logical progression of steps verifying that the plant is fully functional [V, HSE].
- Pre-startup safety review (US regulation, used as a customary reference): mandatory for a new or modified facility; it confirms, before process materials are introduced, conformity with the design, the existence of procedures, the close-out of the recommendations of the hazard analysis and the training of operators [S].

- Reliability-centred maintenance, seven questions in order: functions and expected performance; functional failures; failure modes; effects; consequences; preventive tasks; default action if no task is suitable [S]. The performance targeted is that wanted by the user, not the design capability [S].
- EN 15341: the edition in force arranges the indicators by maintenance sub-functions (assets, safety and environment, management, engineering, organisation, supplies, information systems); the trio "economic, technical, organisational" dates from the 2007 edition [S].
- ISO 14224: nine-level taxonomy, from the industry down to the part [S].
- The European standard knows neither "curative" nor "palliative" maintenance; these terms and the five intervention levels belong to French usage [S].
- ISO 55000 was reissued in 2024 [E].

- The CII guide sets a common vocabulary and scales the effort by means of an interface risk and impact matrix [E].
- Customary vocabulary: the interface point is the boundary at which responsibility passes from one party to another; the interface agreement records the information or deliverable requested and its date [S].
- Figure attributed to the CII — 18% cost growth without formal interface management against 4% with it: not cross-checked [NC]. Do not turn it into an anchor.
- The Crossrail procedure is a real owner's document: it rests on interface control documents, in design and then in construction, drawn up with each interfacing party [V]. Reusable as a template.

- ISO 19650, according to the UK guidance: roles of appointing party and lead appointed party; information states — work in progress, shared, published — with revision control at each state; execution plan drawn up before appointment [V]. Chain of information requirements and master information delivery plan [S].
- ISO 7200: mandatory identifying fields — legal owner, identification number, date of issue, sheet number; the revision index is optional [S, to be cross-checked].
- IEC 61355: three-letter document kind code (technical area, class, subclass) [S].
- ISO 9001:2015 no longer requires a documented procedure for document control [S].
- **The master document register has no dedicated standard**: it is a trade practice.
