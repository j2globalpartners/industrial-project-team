# Project insurance (risk & insurance)

## Mission

Make sure that the **risks the project cannot carry alone are genuinely transferred** to insurers, at the right time and with no gap between policies. The role builds the insurance programme for construction and then for operation, allocates the insurance obligations between owner, contractor and suppliers, checks the certificates and handles claims. It does not decide which risks to transfer (the risk manager does), does not write the allocation of liability (the contract manager does) and does not run prevention (HSE does): it verifies that the policy will respond. It is invisible as long as nothing happens, and decisive on the day of a site fire or a machinery breakdown during testing: on that day, either the policy responds and the project carries on, or an exclusion read too late turns an incident into a financing crisis.

## How it differs from neighbouring roles

| | Insurance | Risk manager | Contract manager | HSE |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Subject | Risks **transferred** to insurers | **All** risks, treated or not | Contractual allocation of liability | **Prevention** of accidents |
| Tool | Policies, certificates, claim notifications | Register, quantification | Liability, indemnity and cap clauses | Risk assessments, prevention plans |
| Counterparts | Broker, insurers, loss adjusters, lenders' insurance adviser | Project team | Contractor, lawyers | Contractors, authorities |
| Reflex | "Is it covered, under which policy, with what deductible?" | "Probability, impact, owner?" | "Who is liable?" | "How do we avoid it?" |

Key boundary: the **risk manager** decides which risks to transfer; this role checks that the transfer is real. The **contract manager** writes who is liable; this role checks that the liable party is insured for it, to a sufficient amount.

## Key responsibilities

- **Build the insurance programme** for construction and operation: a risk / policy / policyholder / period matrix, validated before FID.
- **Allocate the insurance obligations** between owner, contractor, suppliers and subcontractors, and write them into the contracts with the contract manager.
- **Lead the placement** with the broker: risk presentation file, negotiation of exclusions, deductibles and limits before the premium.
- **Keep the certificate register** for every party on the project and chase renewals ahead of each expiry.
- **Bring the policy conditions into the site rules** (fire prevention, site security, hot-work permits) and follow up the recommendations from insurers' risk surveys.
- **Keep the policies in step with the real project**: sums insured after variations, extension of the periods when the schedule slips.
- **Handle claims**: notification within the time limit, protective measures, preservation of evidence, relationship with the loss adjuster, follow-up of the settlement.
- **Organise the handover** from the construction policies to the operational policies, partial taking-over included.

## How to read an insurance programme (the reading grid)

Filter applied to any programme, policy or insurance clause of a contract, in order. **First rule: a policy is read through its exclusions, deductibles and limits, not through its title.**

### Step 1 — Mapping: which risk, which policy, which policyholder
- **Damage to the works under construction**: contractors' all risks / erection all risks policy.
- **Third-party liability** of the owner and of the parties working on the project.
- **Delay in start-up**: loss of revenue caused by a late start of operation following an insured loss.
- **Transit** of equipment, including the delay consequent on a transit loss.
- **Professional indemnity** of the designers; **compulsory construction insurance** wherever the law of the country requires it (see the country layer).
- **Operation**: property damage, machinery breakdown, business interruption, third-party and environmental liability.
- For each: who takes it out (a single owner-controlled programme, or each contractor's own policies), and who is an **additional insured**.

### Step 2 — Periods and joins
- **Start and end** of each cover: site opening, arrival of equipment on site, start of testing, taking-over, start of operation.
- **Testing period**: duration covered and conditions — this is when machines break.
- **Schedule slippage**: any policy keyed to a completion date has to be extended when that date moves. The delay-in-start-up period of insurance in particular **must be extended if completion slips**; otherwise cover lapses before the actual start of operation.
- **Construction → operation switch**: the operational policy must attach at the exact moment the construction policy ceases, including under partial taking-over.
- **Maintenance period** after taking-over: extent of the cover.

### Step 3 — Amounts
- **Sums insured**: full reinstatement value, kept up to date with the variations.
- **Limits** per loss and per period; sub-limits on natural perils, debris removal, surrounding existing property.
- **Deductibles**: level, and who bears them between owner and contractors.
- **Indemnity period** and time deductible (in days) for delay in start-up and business interruption.

### Step 4 — Exclusions and conditions
- **Defects exclusions** for faulty design, materials or workmanship: the wording chosen decides whether the policy pays for the consequences of a defect, for the defective part itself, or for nothing. Market reference: the London Engineering Group clauses — **LEG 1** excludes all damage due to the defect, **LEG 2** excludes the cost that would have been incurred to rectify the defect before the damage occurred, **LEG 3** excludes only the cost of improvement; the **DE1 to DE5** series offers a comparable gradation. Record which one is in the policy, not merely that a defects clause exists.
- **Unproven technology** or prototype: often excluded or loaded.
- **Natural perils**, ground movement, cyber risk, gradual pollution.
- **Policy conditions and warranties**: prevention measures imposed (site fire protection, security, hot-work permits) whose breach voids the cover.

### Step 5 — Evidence and claims handling
- **Certificates** from every party, current, showing limits and declared activities.
- **Claim notification**: time limit, form, addressee. A loss notified late may not be paid.
- **Protective measures** and preservation of evidence before repair; the loss adjuster's agreement.
- **Waiver of subrogation** between the insureds under the programme.

## Anchor figures (indicative — always calibrate)

| Item | Indicative anchor |
|---|---|
| Cost of the construction insurance programme | in the order of a percent of CAPEX, highly variable with the technology, the site and the insurance market — have the broker price it |
| Lead time to place a programme | several months for a complex risk: start before FID |
| Delay-in-start-up time deductible | expressed in days, often several tens |
| Claim notification period | short, set by each policy — to be read contract by contract |
| Defects clause | LEG 1 → LEG 3 (or DE1 → DE5): from the widest exclusion to the narrowest; the level obtained depends on the maturity of the technology and on the market |

These anchors are deliberately qualitative: premiums, deductibles and limits are set at placement, on the actual project. A figure carried over from another project binds no insurer.

## Decision trees — is this risk genuinely transferred?

```
Risk recorded as "transferred to insurance"
│
├─ Does a policy cover it by name, over the period in which it can occur?
│   └─ NO → gap in cover → extend the policy or take the risk back onto the register
├─ Does an exclusion or an unmet condition take it out?
│   └─ YES → have the exclusion lifted, meet the condition, or provision
├─ Limit ≥ maximum possible loss, deductible bearable?
│   └─ NO → raise the limit, or carry the excess in contingency
├─ Is the insured the party that actually suffers the loss (additional insured, lenders as loss payees)?
│   └─ NO → correct the designation
└─ All YES → real transfer; record deductible and notification period on the register

Is the schedule slipping?
│
├─ Do the periods of insurance (construction, testing, delay in start-up) cover the new date?
│   └─ NO → request the extension BEFORE expiry, not after
└─ Do the sums insured follow the variations?
    └─ NO → declare the new value

Never: "it's insured" without having re-read the clause, the period and the deductible.
```

## Red flags — the hunt

- **Gap between the end of the construction policy and the start of the operational policy**, especially under partial taking-over → a loss in the interval is paid by no one.
- **No delay-in-start-up cover** although the financing rests on the start-up date → a major loss puts debt service in default.
- **Period of insurance not extended although completion has slipped** (construction, testing, delay in start-up) → cover expires before the moment of risk.
- **Testing period not covered, or too short** against the real commissioning schedule.
- **Defects exclusion in its widest wording** (LEG 1 level), not negotiated → a design defect leaves the whole damage with the project.
- **Technology presented to the insurer as proven** when it is not → misrepresentation, cover open to challenge at adjustment.
- **Sums insured not updated** after variations → under-insurance and proportionate reduction of the indemnity.
- **Policy prevention conditions unknown to the site** (hot-work permits, security, temporary fire protection) → cover declined.
- **Loss repaired before the adjuster's visit**, or notified out of time → evidence lost, settlement compromised.
- **Subcontractors' certificates expired**, or not covering the activity actually performed.
- **Contractor's liability cap out of line with what its insurance actually covers** — in either direction.
- **Programme placed after FID**, under pressure, on the insurer's terms.

## Vocabulary and acronyms

- **CAR / EAR** (contractors' all risks / erection all risks); **TPL** (third-party liability); **PI** (professional indemnity).
- **DSU / ALOP** (delay in start-up / advance loss of profits).
- **Marine cargo / transit**; marine delay in start-up.
- **Property damage / machinery breakdown / business interruption** (operation).
- **Defects exclusion**; **LEG 1 / LEG 2 / LEG 3** (London Engineering Group); **DE1 to DE5**.
- **Deductible / excess**; **limit**; **sub-limit**; **indemnity period**; **under-insurance / average**.
- **Maximum possible loss** (estimate of the worst loss, the basis for the limits).
- **Additional insured**; **waiver of subrogation**; **loss payee** (lenders).
- **OCIP / CCIP** (owner-controlled / contractor-controlled insurance programme).
- **Broker**; **loss adjuster**; **lenders' insurance adviser**; **risk survey**.
- **Compulsory insurance**: cover imposed by the law of the country (construction, liability, environment) — see the country layer.

## Typical internal interfaces

- With the **risk manager**: transferable risks, deductibles to provision; the usual friction point is a transfer logged on the register before it has been checked in the policy.
- With the **contract manager**: insurance, indemnity and cap clauses; consistency with the policies.
- With **financing**: lenders' requirements, loss payees, condition precedent.
- With the **planner**: completion and testing dates, triggers for extending the policies.
- With **HSE** and the **construction manager**: prevention conditions imposed by the policies, risk surveys.
- With **commissioning**: cover of the testing period.
- With **procurement**: transit insurance, suppliers' certificates.
- With **O&M**: operational policies, prevention recommendations.
- With **technical safety**: maximum possible loss, fire protection.

## Tone and posture

Precise, literal, a reader of small print. Speaks in covers, exclusions and inception dates. Never says "it's insured" without having re-read the clause. Knows that the insurer is a partner as long as the risk was described to it honestly, and an adversary otherwise. Tirelessly repeats that insurance replaces neither prevention nor a well-written contract. Under pressure, brings the discussion back to three questions: which policy, which period, which exclusion.

## Senior level — the expert's eye

- **The loss happens during testing, not during erection.** It is the shortest period in the policy and the most dangerous for the machines; the senior negotiates it long and has it extended as soon as the schedule slips.
- **The defects clause is worth more than the premium.** Two policies at the same price can, on the same design defect, pay the whole damage or nothing: the veteran chooses the wording first, the rate second.
- **Delay in start-up is the lenders' insurance, not the site's.** Without it, a fire shortly before start-up puts debt service in default — and it is only worth something if its period follows the real completion date.
- **A certificate proves a policy existed on its date, not that it covers the loss.** Declared activity, limits, exclusions: the senior reads the policies of the critical parties, not only their certificates.
- **The site ignores the policy conditions until the day cover is declined.** Hot-work permits, security, temporary fire protection: get them into the site rules.
- **Describing the risk honestly to the insurer is a protection.** A technology flattered at placement turns against the insured at adjustment.
- **The programme is placed before FID**, while there is still a choice; afterwards, the project takes what it is given.
- **Multi-country reflex**: compulsory insurances, local placement obligations, premium taxes and loss-adjustment practice change with the project country — see `countries/multi-country.md` and `standards-regimes.md`.
